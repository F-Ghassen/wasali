Visual language now

Uber-style: pure black primary (#000), blue accent (#276EF1), white and #F6F6F6 surfaces, system fonts, emoji icons in stat cards and tabs.

Strengths

Clear role split, auto-filled booking fields, live price summary, draft recovery in the driver wizard, a fully documented state machine, and four locales including Darija.

Character gap

Nothing on screen says Tunisia, family, or travel. The look is borrowed from ride-hailing, so a trust-based diaspora product feels like a generic logistics app.

What the review found

Ranked by effect on the two numbers that matter for a two-sided marketplace: senders reaching a confirmed booking, and drivers publishing a trip.

High

Sign-up comes before any value

The welcome screen routes straight to account creation. A sender cannot see whether anyone is travelling to their city this month.

Fix: show live departures publicly; ask for an account at the booking step.

High

Booking asks for too much, in an accordion

Six steps with four data-entry forms. Re-entry is reduced by saved contacts, but a first booking still means typing two people's details before the driver has said yes.

Fix: three steps (trip, package, hand-off). Collect recipient and address after confirmation when the driver has accepted.

High

Trust is thin for a cash, person-to-person deal

Results show one rating line. Escrow is gone (ADR 0004), so identity, history and proof of hand-off must carry the trust. Docs and parts of the code still mention escrow banners.

Fix: a Trust Passport on every driver, plus photo proof at each hand-off.

Med

The recipient has no seat at the table

Recipients are in Tunisia, often on WhatsApp, and never receive a link. The sender relays every update by hand.

Fix: tokenised tracking link and WhatsApp message, no account needed.

Med

Tab bar mixes the core job with side products

"Ship Docs Fast" (a points-based favour network) shares top-level navigation with Bookings. Requests, the best fallback when search is empty, is hidden from the bar.

Fix: Home, Trips, Inbox, Profile. Requests and Docs become modes inside Home.

Med

Driver app is record-centric, not day-centric

Dashboard counts things. On the day of travel the driver needs an ordered run sheet: where to collect, which weights, which cash to take.

Fix: a Trip Day mode that switches on 48 hours before departure.

Med

Brand and visual language are borrowed

Black, Uber blue and emoji icons. No imagery, colour or type that belongs to the Tunisia–Europe corridor. Emoji render differently per platform and cannot be themed.

Fix: a Mediterranean palette, one display face, and a small custom icon set.

Low

RTL and Darija need first-class layouts

Four locales exist, but the layouts (horizontal country cards, left accent borders, "→" arrows in cities) are written LTR.

Fix: logical properties and mirrored route arrows; review every card in Arabic.

Low

Doc drift

user-flows.md still lists escrow banners and a /booking/review-pay step that the blueprint says no longer exist.

Fix: reconcile in the same commit as any UI change (per CLAUDE.md).

Future UI/UX: send it with someone going there

Design stance: Wasali is a corridor and a community, not a courier. The interface should feel like a departures hall and a trusted neighbour at once. Five concepts, each fixing one or more findings above. Mockups are illustrative; copy and numbers are placeholders.

A. Home is a live departures board

Replace the form-first home with the thing a sender actually wants to know: who is leaving, when, and how much room is left. Filtering is two soft chips, not three required fields.

  • Public without sign-in. Account is requested when tapping Book.
  • Free kilos in plain numbers, red when almost full, so urgency is honest.
  • Empty state turns into the shipping request flow ("Ask the corridor") instead of a dead end.
  • Four tabs: Home, Trips, Inbox, Profile. Documents become a filter chip on the board.
  • Solves: sign-up before value, empty results, tab bar clutter.

B. Book in three steps; details after "yes"

Trip, package, hand-off. The first booking is a request. Recipient details and addresses are collected after the driver confirms, when the sender has a reason to give them.

  • The driver's prohibited items are shown beside the category picker, not buried in route detail.
  • Price sticks to the bottom and names who gets paid and when.
  • Saved recipients appear as one-tap chips after confirmation.
  • Hand-off (meeting point or door) is a single choice with a map, not two separate logistics blocks.
  • Solves: booking length, abandoned first bookings.

C. Hand-off moments are the product

With cash and no escrow, trust is built by evidence. Each hand-off becomes a recorded moment (weight, photo, who, when), and the recipient joins through a link, not an app.

  • Recipient gets a WhatsApp message with a tokenised page: status, driver photo, a QR to show on delivery.
  • "Report issue" is available at every stage, not only after delivery, and starts with the photo proof already attached.
  • Ratings are asked at the hand-off, in a two-tap sheet, while the memory is fresh.
  • Solves: thin trust, recipient blind spot, late disputes.

D. Drivers get a run sheet, not counters

The dashboard turns into "Today" once a trip is within 48 hours: ordered stops, kilos used against luggage, and exactly which cash to collect where.

  • One primary action per moment: Scan next parcel, then Re-weigh, then Mark paid.
  • Large touch targets for use at a station or car boot, with an offline-safe queue for patchy signal.
  • Publishing a trip is a 60-second "Repeat last trip" path; the five-step wizard stays for new routes.
  • Earnings show as a trip receipt ("this trip paid €310") rather than a chart first.
  • Solves: record-centric dashboard, driver retention.

E. A Trust Passport on every driver

One shareable page gathers everything that makes a stranger safe to hand a parcel to. Verified facts are separated from opinions, and each verification says what was checked.

  • Verification chips: ID checked, phone, and proof of ticket for this trip.
  • Real hand-off photos from past bookings, blurred for privacy where needed.
  • Shareable link so drivers bring their own audience from WhatsApp and Facebook groups, where the corridor already lives.
  • Compact version appears on every board row; the full page opens from the route.
  • Solves: one-line rating, driver acquisition.

Type

Send it with someone going there.

A sturdy serif for display moments (headings, city names, the board) paired with the platform sans for UI text. The pair needs an Arabic counterpart with matching weight; test with IBM Plex Sans Arabic or Noto Naskh before committing.

Component moves

Departure-board rows replace generic route cards. Status becomes a four-dot hand-off trail rather than a coloured badge. Emoji tab and stat icons become a single custom line-icon set. Corner radii step: 8 for inputs, 14 for cards, full for pills; never one radius on everything.

A sensible order to build

Sequenced so each phase ships value on its own and reuses the stores and services already in place. Per the project rules, each feature gets components / hooks / services / utils / types.

Phase 1 · quick wins

Trust and clarity

Public browsing before sign-up. Trust chips on route cards. Surface Requests as the empty-results fallback. Remove stale escrow copy. Reconcile docs.

Phase 2 · core

Shorter booking and hand-offs

Three-step booking, delayed recipient step. Hand-off photo proof on collect and deliver. Recipient WhatsApp link (new edge function and a token column; follows the schema workflow).

Phase 3 · identity

Brand and drivers

New tokens, departures board home, Trust Passport page, Trip Day mode, "Repeat last trip", RTL pass, custom icon set.

How to judge it

Sender: share of visitors who reach Book without an account, and share of first bookings confirmed by the driver. Driver: time to publish a repeat trip, and share of bookings completed without a dispute. Test A and B with 5 senders each in Paris and Lyon, and C and D with 5 drivers before building them in full.
